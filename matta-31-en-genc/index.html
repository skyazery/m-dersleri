Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 31

The Darkest Night

Matthew 26:36–75

From Thursday night to Friday morning. In the garden of the oil press, Jesus falls to the ground and prays, is betrayed with a kiss, undergoes an illegal trial, and is denied three times by his closest disciple. On that night when everyone abandoned him, Jesus held on to the Father's will all alone.

Background

We understand Jesus's true humanity and obedience in his Gethsemane prayer.

Main idea

We can explain Jesus's voluntary suffering and identity in the arrest and trial scenes.

Application

We learn the path of repentance after failure through Peter's denial and tears.

1 · Matthew 26:36–46

This Cup

“My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even to death.” In , Jesus falls on his face and prays three times: “My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass away from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will.”

The “cup” is God's cup of wrath {Isa. 51:17}. The Sinless One had to bear the place of sinners. The three disciples fell asleep three times. “The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.”

Looking Deeper · Two Gardens

In the Garden of Eden, Adam did “his own will” and turned away from God. In the Garden of Gethsemane, Jesus, the second Adam, does “the Father's will” and brings us back to God. “Your will be done” from the Lord's Prayer {Matt. 6:10} is prayed in the heaviest moment.

2 · Matthew 26:47–56

A Kiss and a Sword

Judas said, “Greetings, Rabbi!” and kissed him. Jesus said, “Friend, why are you here?” He called him “friend” to the very end.

One disciple (Peter, according to the Gospel of John) cut off the ear of the high priest's servant with a sword. Jesus said, “All those who take the sword will die by the sword.” Even though he could have called more than twelve legions of , he let himself be arrested so the Scriptures would be fulfilled. “Then all the disciples left him, and fled.”

Looking Deeper · Voluntary Suffering

Jesus was not arrested helplessly. He could have called twelve legions (about 72,000) of angels, but chose not to. He lived out his own words from {Matt. 5:39}, “don't resist him who is evil,” with his body facing the cross.

3 · Matthew 26:57–68

An Illegal Trial

The trial before the held in the middle of the night was illegal even according to the law of that time. The words of the false witnesses did not agree with each other. Jesus remained silent, just as in {Isa. 53:7}.

When the high priest said, “I adjure you by the living God ... are you the Christ?” Jesus answered: “You have said it. ... Nevertheless, you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of Power, and coming on the clouds of the sky.” This answer combines {Dan. 7:13} and {Ps. 110:1}.

Looking Deeper · The Judge on Trial

Jesus reveals that he is the “Son of Man” from {Dan. 7}, the One who will judge the whole world. The one being judged is actually the Judge of the last day. They call this confession “blasphemy” and sentence him to death.

4 · Matthew 26:69–75

A Rooster's Crow and Tears

Peter still followed. While the other disciples fled, he came as far as the courtyard. But he fell before a servant girl's question. First, “I don't know what you are talking about,” then with an oath, and finally cursing, “I don't know the man.”

At the same time, Jesus was confessing, while Peter was denying. When the rooster crowed, Peter remembered Jesus's words and “went out and wept bitterly.” Both Judas and Peter stumbled, but Peter's tears led to , opening the way to restoration.

Looking Deeper · Judas and Peter

Both betrayed Jesus. Judas felt remorse {Matt. 27:3}, but did not return to Jesus. Peter cried and eventually returned {John 21}. The difference is not in the size of the sin, but in where they turned after falling.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Jesus also prayed honestly in his suffering. Do I tell God my honest feelings?
  2. “The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.” In what area of my faith does this saying apply the most?
  3. Have I ever said “I don't know him” about Jesus in front of my friends?
  4. What makes the difference between collapsing alone like Judas and returning like Peter after falling?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, I confess that the suffering you endured—holding on to the Father's will all alone in the darkest night, being betrayed by someone you called a friend, and being judged in a false trial—was for me. Let me seek the Father's will more than my own desires. When I stumble like Peter, let me not hide, but weep and return to you. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
