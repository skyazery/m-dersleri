Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 4

Repentance and the Opened Heavens

Matthew 3:1–17

By the river where everyone came to confess their sins, a sinless One stood in the same line with them. Why was Jesus baptized?

Tap the underlined words: meaning, original-language form and Azerbaijani word.

Background

We understand the meaning of "repentance" and "the kingdom of heaven" preached by John the Baptist.

Main idea

We can explain that God the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit appeared together at Jesus' baptism.

Application

We think about what the words "beloved Son" mean for our identity.

1 · Matthew 3:1–6

A Voice in the Wilderness

wore clothes made of camel's hair and had a leather belt around his waist. This was the appearance of the prophet Elijah in the Old Testament 2 Kings 1:8. After four hundred years without a prophet, a prophet appeared again in Israel.

He was the "voice of one crying in the wilderness" spoken of by Isa. 40:3. His message was short: ", for has come near."

Looking Deeper · Why the wilderness?

The wilderness is the place where Israel stayed for 40 years after leaving Egypt and came to know God. John called the people back to the wilderness so they could make a fresh start as they crossed the Jordan River. God started His new work not in a magnificent temple, but in the empty desert.

2 · Matthew 3:7–12

Show It with Fruit

John called the and Sadducees who came to be baptized a "brood of vipers!" and told them to "produce fruit worthy of repentance."

"Don't think to yourselves, 'We have Abraham as our father.'" This means that a good family or religious background does not guarantee salvation.

Looking Deeper · Fruitless Religion

John's warning was not directed at those outside the faith, but at the most religious people. Going to church for a long time and growing up in a believing family are good things, but they cannot replace repentance.

3 · Matthew 3:13–15

The Baptism of the Sinless One

When Jesus came to be baptized, John tried to stop Him: "I need to be baptized by You, and do You come to me?"

Jesus said, "Let it be so now, for it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness in this way." Jesus stood in the same line as sinners.

Looking Deeper · "Fulfilling all righteousness"

Jesus had no sin to confess, but He identified Himself with sinners. This scene foreshadows the cross: a sinless One standing in the place of sinners. This is exactly what the Gospel is.

4 · Matthew 3:16–17

The Opened Heavens, the Heard Voice

When Jesus came up out of the water, the heavens opened, the descended like a dove, and a voice was heard: "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased."

These words combine Psalm 2:7 (the Kingly Son) and Isaiah 42:1 (the suffering servant). Jesus is both the King and the suffering Servant.

Looking Deeper · Love Before Ministry

These words were spoken before Jesus had performed even a single miracle. God's love is not a reward given for success. Identity comes first, and then comes the mission.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. If repentance is "changing direction", what direction do I need to turn away from now?
  2. Have I ever felt complacent just because "my family are believers"?
  3. How does the fact that Jesus stood in the same line as sinners comfort me?
  4. When is it hard to believe in God who calls me His "beloved child" even when I haven't done anything well?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Lord God, thank You that the sinless Jesus stood in the place of sinners. Help me to repent in a way that changes the direction of my life, not just with words. Help me to stand on the truth that I am Your beloved child, not on my successes. I pray in the name of Your beloved Son, Jesus Christ. Amen.
