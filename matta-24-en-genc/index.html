Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 24

What do I still lack?

Matthew 19:1–30

A young man who had everything asked, 'What do I still lack?' Jesus looked not at what he had, but at what had him. In the same scene, children who have nothing receive a blessing, while the young man who has everything goes away sad.

Background

Through the contrast between the children and the rich young man, we understand the way to enter the kingdom of God.

Main idea

We can explain the principle of salvation expressed in the phrase 'a camel and the eye of a needle'.

Application

We look at what 'wealth' holds us back and decide to follow Him.

1 · Matthew 19:1–15

Those who have nothing

Jesus leaves Galilee and goes to Judea across the Jordan. The final journey toward the cross begins. The Pharisees tested Him with the issue of divorce, but Jesus goes back to the beginning of creation and speaks of the sanctity of marriage.

Then comes the scene with the children. The disciples hindered them, but Jesus says, 'The kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these.' The children have nothing to show off, so they are able to receive. This connects with 18:3.

A Deeper Look · Marriage and singleness

In 19:3–12, based on Genesis 1–2, Jesus says that marriage is something God has joined together, while also stating that singleness for the kingdom of God is valuable. Both paths are lives dedicated to God.

2 · Matthew 19:16–22

One thing lacking

The young man asked, 'What thing must I do to get eternal life?' He thought of eternal life as an 'achievement to be done.' Jesus quotes the commandments regarding neighbors from the Ten Commandments and adds the command to 'love your neighbor as yourself.'

'I have kept all these, what do I still lack?' Jesus says, 'If you want to be perfect, go, sell your possessions, give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me.' Because the young man had great , he went away sad.

A Deeper Look · The first commandment

Jesus only mentioned the commandments related to neighbors. Then, with the words 'sell your possessions,' He tested the first commandment ('You shall have no other gods before me'). The young man's true god was his wealth. The words in 6:24, 'You cannot serve both God and wealth,' became a reality.

3 · Matthew 19:23–26

Impossible for man

'It is easier for a to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God.' The camel was the largest animal in Palestine, and the eye of a needle was the smallest hole. It is an intentional impossibility.

At that time, wealth was considered proof of God's blessing. Therefore, the disciples were amazed and asked, 'Who then can be saved?' Jesus' answer: 'With men this is impossible, but with God all things are possible.' Salvation is not an achievement, but God's work.

A Deeper Look · 'The Eye of the Needle Gate'?

There is an explanation that there was a small gate in Jerusalem called the 'eye of a needle,' and a camel had to unload its burden and kneel to pass through it, but there is no historical basis for this. Jesus said 'impossible,' not 'difficult,' meaning that only God can do it.

4 · Matthew 19:27–30

The first and the last

Peter said, 'See, we have left everything and followed You. What then will we have?' They were people who made the opposite choice of the young man.

Jesus promises that in the new world they will sit on twelve thrones, and will receive a of what they left behind, and eternal life. But at the end, He gives a warning: 'Many who are first will be last, and many who are last will be first.' This leads into the parable of the vineyard in the next chapter.

A Deeper Look · Is it wrong to ask for a reward?

Jesus does not scold Peter for his question, but answers with a promise. God is not one who gives nothing in return. But He shows in the next chapter that the reward is 'grace,' not a 'calculation.'

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Before Jesus, am I closer to the 'child who has nothing' or the 'young man who has everything'?
  2. What is something I cannot easily let go of, like the rich young man's 'wealth'?
  3. In what situation do I need the words, 'With men this is impossible, but with God all things are possible'?
  4. Have I ever given up something for Jesus? What did I gain afterward?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for hugging and blessing the children who had nothing to show off. Show me the 'wealth' that holds me back, and instead of going away sad, help me to joyfully let it go and follow You. I believe that what is impossible for man is possible for God. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
