Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 15

The One Who Gives Rest

Matthew 11:1–30

Even the greatest prophet hesitated in prison. John the Baptist asked, “Are You really the One?” To people exhausted by doubt and weariness, Jesus offers an invitation before giving an answer: come to Me.

Tap the underlined words: meaning, original-language form and Azerbaijani word.

Background

We understand the identity of the Messiah through John the Baptist's question and Jesus' answer.

Main idea

We can explain what it means that God's Kingdom is revealed to 'little children'.

Application

We apply the rest that comes from taking Jesus' yoke and learning from Him to our lives.

1 · Matthew 11:1–15

John Hesitates

John had said the Messiah would judge with an ax and fire (3:10–12). But Jesus was healing the sick and eating with sinners. In prison, John was confused.

Jesus answers by combining Isaiah 35 and 61: the blind see, the lame walk, and the is preached to the poor. And He says: “Blessed is he who doesn't stumble because of Me!”

Look Deeper · He Didn't Scold the Doubt

Right after John's question, Jesus praises him, saying, 'Among those born of women there has not risen anyone greater than John.' Doubt is not the opposite of faith. John took his doubt and went to Jesus. That is faith.

2 · Matthew 11:16–24

Children in the Market

Jesus compares that generation to children refusing to play: they won't join a wedding game or a funeral game. John fasted, and they said, 'He has a demon'; Jesus ate and drank, and they said, 'He is a glutton.'

Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum, which saw the most miracles, did not repent. Jesus sighs over these cities. Those who see much bear much responsibility.

Look Deeper · The Fruit of Wisdom

“Wisdom is proved right by her actions” (11:19). Truth is proven by fruit, not by people's criticism. This connects to the words in chapter 7, “by their fruit you will recognize them.”

3 · Matthew 11:25–27

Revealed to Little Children

“I thank You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that You have hidden these things from the wise and understanding, and revealed them to .” At the moment of rejection, Jesus gives thanks instead of complaining.

“No one knows the Son except the Father. No one knows the Father except the Son, and anyone to whom the Son desires to reveal Him.” The way to know God is opened only through Jesus.

Look Deeper · Revelation

Knowing God is not something 'achieved' by studying, but something God 'shows'. Therefore, a humble person has the advantage. Knowledge is valuable, but proud knowledge blinds the eyes.

4 · Matthew 11:28–30

Take My Yoke

“Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened.” At that time, the Pharisees added hundreds of rules to the Law, crushing the people (23:4).

Jesus doesn't make the burden disappear; He changes the . A yoke is carried by two oxen together. When we carry it with Jesus, He takes the weight, and we 'learn' from Him. “I am gentle and humble in heart; take My yoke upon you and learn from Me.”

Look Deeper · Rest (Anapausis)

In the Old Testament, 'rest' refers to the Sabbath and the Promised Land. The very next chapter, 12, is about a Sabbath dispute. Jesus is the Lord of the Sabbath who gives the true rest of the Sabbath.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Have you ever doubted like John, asking, 'Is Jesus really the One?' What did you do then?
  2. When are the times I act like I 'don't dance when the flute is played'?
  3. What is the 'burden' that makes me most tired right now? Whose yoke is this?
  4. What might 'learning' from Jesus look like in my daily life?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for not scolding John when he doubted, and for calling the weary to 'come to Me'. Let me lay down the heavy burden in my heart and take Your yoke. Let me find true rest by learning from You, who are gentle and humble. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
