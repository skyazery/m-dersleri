Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 19

Jesus, Not the Wind

Matthew 14:1–36

A day that began with the sorrow of John's death. Jesus wanted to rest, but He fed the crowds that flocked to Him, prayed all night, and came trampling the storm towards morning. And He reached out His hand to Peter, who was sinking.

Background

We understand what the kingdom of heaven looks like by comparing Herod's banquet with Jesus' banquet.

Main idea

We can explain what walking on the water says about Jesus' identity.

Application

We practice fixing our eyes on Jesus in the storms of our lives.

1 · Matthew 14:1–14

Two banquets

John the Baptist was killed at Herod Antipas' birthday banquet. It was a banquet where the ruler's prestige and oath were more important than the life of a righteous man.

Hearing the news, Jesus went to a deserted place to be alone. He needed time to grieve. But when the crowd followed Him, Jesus was not annoyed; He on them. Even in His sorrow, His love did not stop.

Looking deeper · The palace and the wilderness

The banquet in the palace brought death, while the banquet in the wilderness brought life. Matthew puts the two stories side by side to show who the “true King” is.

2 · Matthew 14:15–21

You give them something to eat

The disciples offered a realistic solution: “Send the crowds away.” Jesus says, “They don't need to go away. You give them something to eat.” He entrusted the impossible to the disciples.

“He took them, looked up to heaven, , broke the loaves, and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.” These verbs (take, give thanks, break, give) are repeated exactly at the Last Supper (26:26).

Looking deeper · Manna in the wilderness

Feeding the people in the wilderness recalls the manna in the book of Exodus. The twelve baskets may symbolize the twelve tribes. Jesus is more than a new Moses; He is God who feeds His people.

3 · Matthew 14:22–27

“I AM” on the water

Jesus sent the crowds away, went up on the mountain by Himself, and prayed. Meanwhile, the disciples were fighting a headwind all night. “The fourth watch of the night” is between 3 and 6 a.m. Jesus came at the most exhausting and darkest time.

“Cheer up! , do not be afraid!” In Greek, it is “ego eimi”, meaning “I am”. This sounds the same as the name by which God revealed Himself to Moses as “I AM WHO I AM” (Exodus 3:14).

Looking deeper · Trampling the sea

Job 9:8 calls God the one who “tramples the waves of the sea”. In the Old Testament, the sea is a symbol of chaos. Jesus is the Creator walking on that chaos.

4 · Matthew 14:28–36

Little faith, a catching hand

Peter boldly stepped out onto the water. The other disciples stayed in the boat. But “he saw the wind and was afraid” and began to sink. The situation was the same, but his focus changed.

Jesus “immediately” reached out His hand and caught him. “You of little faith, why did you doubt?” The hand came before the rebuke. For the first time, those in the boat Him, saying, “You are truly the Son of God.”

Looking deeper · A failed Peter?

Peter sank, but he was the only disciple who walked on the water. Only the one who steps out of the boat experiences failure. Jesus catches him even as he sinks. Doubt (distazo) means “being double-minded”.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Jesus had compassion on people even in His sorrow. Can I care for others when I am having a hard time?
  2. Like “You give them something to eat”, is there a task Jesus has entrusted to me that seems beyond my ability?
  3. What is the “wind” that is making me sink these days?
  4. Have I ever had a faith experience of “stepping out of the boat” like Peter?

My confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, I praise You for having compassion on the crowd even in Your sorrow and for feeding so many with so little. Let me hear Your voice coming over the storm, saying, “Cheer up, it is I.” Let me look at You instead of the wind, and trust in Your hand that catches me when I sink. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
