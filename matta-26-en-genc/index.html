Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 26

Which King Are We Welcoming?

Matthew 21:1–22

A cheering crowd, coats spread out, palm branches waving. But the King is coming on a donkey, not a warhorse. Five days later, this same city will shout, “Crucify Him!” Which King are we expecting?

Tap the underlined words: meaning, original-language form and Azerbaijani word.

Background

We understand the meaning of the entry into Jerusalem fulfilling Zech. 9:9.

Main idea

We can explain the combined message of the cleansing of the temple and the cursing of the fig tree.

Application

We examine whether our worship and faith are just “leaves” or true “fruit.”

1 · Matthew 21:1–11

Humble and Riding on a Donkey

Just before the Passover, Jerusalem was full of pilgrims. Jesus intentionally had a prepared. Matthew quotes Zech. 9:9: “Tell the daughter of Zion, ‘Behold, your King comes to you, humble, and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey.’”

The crowd sings Ps. 118: “ to the Son of David.” But the people of the city ask, “Who is this?” and the crowd answers, “This is the prophet, Jesus, from Nazareth of Galilee.” They still didn't fully know who He was.

Looking Deeper · The King of Peace

A king who won a war would ride a warhorse into the city. A donkey is the mount of a king of peace (1 Kings 1:33 with Solomon). Zech. 9:10 continues: “He will cut off the chariot... and the battle bow will be cut off; and he will speak peace to the nations.” The crowd expected a king who would drive out Rome, but Jesus was a different kind of King.

2 · Matthew 21:12–13

A Den of Robbers

The Court of the Gentiles was the only place where foreigners could also pray to God. But it was full of people selling sacrificial animals and money changers. Because the temple tax could only be paid with a special Tyrian silver coin, they charged exchange fees.

Jesus quotes Isa. 56:7 (“My house shall be called a house of prayer”) and Jer. 7:11 (“a den of robbers”) together. A den of robbers isn't where the robbery happens, but where the robbers hide. It is an accusation that the temple had become a place hiding injustice behind religious rituals.

Looking Deeper · Jesus' Anger

Jesus' anger is not for Himself, but for God's house and for the foreigners and the poor who were being pushed out of it. In the very next verse, Jesus heals the blind and the lame in that same temple. After overturning it, He restores it.

3 · Matthew 21:14–17

The Children's Hosanna

According to tradition, it was difficult for the blind and the lame to enter deep into the temple (2 Sam. 5:8). But Jesus heals them right inside that temple. The temple returned to its true purpose.

The children shouted the song they had learned on the road inside the temple. The chief priests were furious, but Jesus quotes Ps. 8:2: “Out of the mouth of children and nursing babies you have perfected .” The religious leaders didn't see it, but the children did.

Looking Deeper · The Fulfillment of 11:25

“I thank you... that you hid these things from the wise and understanding, and revealed them to infants.” The prayer from chapter 11 came true exactly like this in the temple.

4 · Matthew 21:18–22

A Tree with Only Leaves

When a sprouts leaves, it also begins to produce small early fruits (page). A tree with abundant leaves but no fruit is in a state of “looking like it has something, but having nothing.” At Jesus' word, the tree withered at once.

This action is an “acted parable” that explains the previous temple scene. A temple with grand worship and many sacrifices, but without the fruit God is looking for (justice, mercy, prayer). Then Jesus teaches His disciples about praying with unwavering faith.

Looking Deeper · Why Curse the Tree?

Jesus wasn't just taking out His anger. Old Testament prophets (Jer. 8:13; Hos. 9:10) compared Israel to a fruitless fig tree. Jesus was prophesying with an action instead of words. The warning applies to us as well.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. What kind of “King” am I expecting from Jesus? A King who will solve my problems, or a King who will change me?
  2. What “tables need to be overturned” in our church or in my worship?
  3. Why might the simple praise of children be deeper than that of adults?
  4. In what area of my faith do I have many “leaves” (what is visible) but little “fruit”?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, the humble King who came riding on a donkey, hosanna! Please rule over me not according to my expectations, but in Your way. Cleanse my heart to be a house of prayer, and help me to bear the fruit of justice and mercy, rather than having a faith with only leaves. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
