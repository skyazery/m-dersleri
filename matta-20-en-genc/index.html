Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 20

Even a Crumb is Enough

Matthew 15:1–39

It was a time that divided people into “clean” and “unclean.” The standard was whether hands were washed and what nation one belonged to. Jesus erases that boundary and finds a faith in an outsider Gentile woman that He had not seen in Israel.

Background

We understand the difference between human tradition and God's commandment, and between the outside and the inside.

Main idea

We can explain the characteristics of faith in the story of the Canaanite woman.

Application

We look at the “inside” and “outside” boundaries we draw and decide to pray persistently.

1 · Matthew 15:1–9

The tradition of the elders

The Pharisees' hand washing was not about hygiene, but a religious purification ritual. It was the that extended a rule meant only for priests in the Law to everyone.

Jesus asks a counter-question: isn't the tradition of swearing “Corban” (dedicated to God) to avoid honoring parents canceling God's commandment? Then He quotes Isaiah 29:13: “These people honor me with their lips, but their is far from me.”

Looking deeper · Is tradition bad?

Tradition itself can be good. The problem arises when human tradition is placed above God's commandment. Church culture must face the same question: does this tradition bring me closer to God, or does it hide God?

2 · Matthew 15:10–20

What comes from inside

“Not that which goes into the mouth defiles a man; but that which comes out of the mouth, this defiles a man.” Jesus moves the place of purity from the hands to the .

“For out of the heart come evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, sexual sins, thefts, false testimony, and blasphemies.” The problem is not in the outside world, but inside me.

Looking deeper · The blind leading the blind

Jesus calls the Pharisees “blind leading the blind.” A person who cannot see their own heart cannot guide others on the right path. This connects to the story of the “plank” in 7:3–5.

3 · Matthew 15:21–28

The faith on the farthest edge

Jesus went to the Gentile region of Tyre and Sidon. The woman was a Gentile, a woman, and the mother of a demon-possessed daughter. Moreover, “” was an ancient enemy of Israel in the Old Testament. Such a woman called Jesus “Son of David.”

Despite Jesus' silence and words that sounded like a rejection, the woman did not back down. “Yes, Lord, but even the dogs eat the that fall from their masters' table.” Jesus is amazed: “Woman, great is your faith.” This contrasts with the “you of little faith” spoken to Peter in 14:31.

Looking deeper · Why did He reject her at first?

The sequence of Jesus' mission (Israel first, 10:6) is visible, but the conversation itself becomes a stage to reveal the woman's faith. The woman claims no rights, relying only on grace. It is a faith that says even a “crumb” is enough. Because Jesus' grace is so great that even a crumb will overflow.

4 · Matthew 15:29–39

A feast in the land of the Gentiles

East of Galilee (likely in the Decapolis, a Gentile region), Jesus healed many sick people, and the people “glorified the God of Israel.” Gentiles are praising the God of Israel.

Then He feeds four thousand people. While the five thousand in chapter 14 were in Jewish territory, this time it is in Gentile territory. The “crumb” mentioned by the Canaanite woman has now become an overflowing feast for the Gentiles.

Looking deeper · 12 and 7

After the five thousand, twelve baskets were left over (the twelve tribes of Israel), and after the four thousand, seven baskets were left over. 7 is a number of perfection, or it symbolizes all nations, recalling the seven nations of Canaan (Deut. 7:1).

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. In which of my faith habits has “the habit itself” become more important than God?
  2. If I remember the words that have come out of my mouth recently, what is the condition of my heart?
  3. Does our church or do I divide people into “inside” and “outside”?
  4. Is there a topic I continue to pray about even though I feel like I've been rejected?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for looking at the heart, not the outside. Let me love God with my heart, not just my lips. Erase the boundaries I have drawn, and give me great faith to ask to the end, relying on grace rather than rights, like the Canaanite woman. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
