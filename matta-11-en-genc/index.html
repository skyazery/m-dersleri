Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 11

Hearing and Doing

Matthew 7:1–29

This is the final chapter of the Sermon on the Mount. Jesus says that just hearing beautiful words and being moved by them is not enough. In the end, there is only one question: are you building your life on these words?

Background

We understand the teachings on judging, prayer, and the Golden Rule.

Main idea

We can explain the 'two paths, two trees, two houses' structure, which is the conclusion of the Sermon on the Mount.

Application

We will make a plan to turn a word we have heard into action this week.

1 · Matthew 7:1–6

The speck and the plank

‘Don't , so that you won't be judged.’ Jesus, the son of a carpenter, paints an exaggerated picture: a man with a roof plank in his eye wants to take the speck out of his friend's eye.

This doesn't mean we shouldn't use discernment. The very next verse says, ‘Don't give that which is holy to the dogs.’ The problem is , where a person ignores their own faults and only measures others.

Looking Deeper · The same measure

‘With whatever measure you measure, it will be measured to you.’ A person who is harsh to others will stand before the same standard. A person who has received grace looks at others through the measure of grace.

2 · Matthew 7:7–12

Ask, seek, knock

All three verbs are in a present tense that implies ‘continuously.’ Keep asking, keep seeking, and keep knocking.

‘If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father who is in heaven give good things to those who ask him!’ Then comes the summary of the Sermon on the Mount: the .

Looking Deeper · The Golden Rule

Rabbi Hillel of that time taught, ‘Do not do to others what is hateful to you.’ Jesus turned this into a positive command: ‘Whatever you desire for people to do to you, you shall also do to them.’ It shifts from an avoiding love to a proactive, approaching love.

3 · Matthew 7:13–23

Two paths and two trees

‘Enter in by the narrow gate; for wide is the gate and broad is the way that leads to destruction, and many are those who enter in by it.’ The path of life is not determined by a popularity vote.

False prophets are known by their . Even if they say ‘Lord, Lord’ and perform miracles, if they don't do the will of the Heavenly Father, Jesus will say: ‘I never knew you.’

Looking Deeper · I never knew you

The most terrifying phrase is not ‘you didn't know Me,’ but ‘I didn't know you.’ Faith is not a checklist of religious activities; it is a relationship with Jesus.

4 · Matthew 7:24–29

The house on the rock

In Galilee, during the dry season, a dry riverbed looked flat and solid. But when the sudden floods came in the rainy season, a house built on it would be washed away.

The two houses looked identical on the outside. The difference only became apparent when the storm hit. The one who hears and ‘does’ is the person who builds their house on the . The crowds were astonished that Jesus taught with , unlike the teachers of the law.

Looking Deeper · Summary of the Sermon on the Mount

Chapter 5 Beatitudes → Chapter 5 Fulfillment of the Law → Chapter 6 Secret piety and trust → Chapter 7 Relationships and decision. The Sermon on the Mount is not a ‘do this and you will be saved’ manual, but a map showing ‘this is how the people of the kingdom of heaven live.’

Discussion questions

  1. When do I often see 'someone else's speck'? What is my 'plank' in that moment?
  2. If I apply the Golden Rule at school or home today, what action would that be?

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for showing the life of the people of the kingdom of heaven through the Sermon on the Mount. Help me to see my own plank before condemning others, and to persistently ask the Father.
