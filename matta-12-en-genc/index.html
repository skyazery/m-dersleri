Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 12

The Authority of the Word

Matthew 8:1–34

Those who heard the Sermon on the Mount were amazed, saying, “He teaches as one having authority.” Chapter 8 shows that this authority is not only in words. Sickness, distance, nature, and evil spirits all bow down before a single word from Jesus.

Background

We understand that the miracles in chapter 8 show the authority of the Word.

Main idea

We can explain the meaning of Jesus approaching the marginalized (people with leprosy, Gentiles, women).

Application

We confess our trust in Jesus during the “storms” of our lives.

1 · Matthew 8:1–4

Touching the Untouchable

According to the law, a person with had to live outside the camp, crying out, “Unclean, unclean” {Lev. 13:45–46}. Anyone who touched him also became unclean.

Jesus could have healed him with just a word, but He intentionally reached out His hand. The uncleanness did not transfer to Jesus; instead, Jesus' transferred to him.

Look Deeper · Show yourself to the priest

Jesus sent the healed man to the priest to offer the gift according to Moses' rule {Lev. 14}. This was the official way to return to society. Jesus restored not only his body but also his relationships.

2 · Matthew 8:5–13

The Faith of a Gentile

The was an officer of the occupying Roman army, meaning he was a Gentile. He said, “I am also a man under authority. I tell this one, ‘Go,’ and he goes; and tell another, ‘Come,’ and he comes.” He understood that Jesus' word changes reality like a command.

Jesus said, “I haven't found such great faith with anyone in Israel.” Many will come from the east and the west, and will sit down with Abraham at the banquet in the kingdom of heaven.

Look Deeper · An Open Banquet

Although the Gospel of Matthew was written for Jews, Gentiles appear at the beginning (the wise men), the middle (the centurion, the Canaanite woman), and the end (all nations). The door to the banquet of God's Kingdom is open to all nations.

3 · Matthew 8:14–22

He Took Our Weaknesses

When Peter's mother-in-law was healed from the fever, she got up and Jesus. This same word is used in the scene where angels served Jesus {Matt. 4:11}. A healed person becomes a serving person.

Matthew quotes Isaiah 53:4: “He took our weaknesses, and bore our diseases.” The prophecy about the Suffering Servant is first fulfilled in His healing ministry.

Look Deeper · The Cost of Following

In {Matt. 8:18–22}, Jesus says, “The foxes have holes, and the birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head.” He lets those who saw the miracles know in advance that following Him is not an easy path.

4 · Matthew 8:23–34

He Rebuked the Wind and the Sea

The Sea of Galilee is 200 meters below sea level and surrounded by mountains, so violent winds suddenly arise. It was a storm so strong that even the disciples, who were fishermen, felt they were going to die.

Jesus said, “Why are you fearful, O you of little faith?” and rebuked the wind and the sea. In the Old Testament, only God calms the sea {Ps. 107:29}. The disciples' question, “What kind of man is this?” is the question of the entire Gospel of Matthew.

Look Deeper · The Region of Gadara

Crossing the lake, Jesus heals two demon-possessed men in Gadara, a Gentile land {Matt. 8:28–34}. Both nature and evil spirits submit to Jesus' authority. But the townspeople cared more about losing their pigs and begged Jesus to leave.

Discussion questions

  1. Who is the “untouchable” person around us today?
  2. What challenges me the most about the centurion's faith?
  3. What is the “storm” in my life these days? Are there times when I feel like Jesus is “sleeping”?
  4. Have I ever cared more about my own “pigs” (losses) than about Jesus, like the people of Gadara?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, I bow down to Your authority that commands sickness, distance, nature, and evil spirits. Touch me too with the hands that touched the marginalized. Let me believe in Your word like the centurion, and trust that You are with me even in the storm. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
