Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 6

Leaving the Nets

Matthew 4:12–25

In those days, a student would choose a teacher they respected and go to them. Jesus, on the contrary, went Himself to the fishermen of Galilee, whom no one else would choose, and called them.

Background

We understand the meaning of Jesus starting His ministry in Galilee.

Main idea

We can explain the nature of the call to discipleship and the cost of following.

Application

We find what we can do in our own lives as “fishers of men.”

1 · Matthew 4:12–17

Light Dawning in the Darkness

After John was arrested, Jesus moved to and began His ministry. Matthew quotes Isaiah 9:1–2: “The people who sat in darkness saw a great light.”

was a remote area where various nations lived, so much so that it was called “Galilee of the Gentiles.” The light fell first on the outskirts, not on the elite of Jerusalem.

A Deeper Look · Why Galilee?

The land of Zebulun and Naphtali was the first place trampled by Assyria in the 8th century BC. The light came first to the place that fell into darkness first. God begins restoration where the wound is deepest.

2 · Matthew 4:18–20

The Teacher Calls Himself

At that time, to become a of a , a student would go to the teacher and ask to be accepted. But Jesus approached the working fishermen Himself and said, “Follow Me.”

Peter and Andrew “immediately” left their nets and followed Him. Matthew emphasizes this word “immediately” twice.

A Deeper Look · Fishers of Men

Jesus turned what they were good at, catching fish, into a new mission. The call sometimes requires us to leave what we have, and sometimes it calls us to use it in a new way for the kingdom of God.

3 · Matthew 4:21–22

Leaving the Boat and Father

James and John were mending their nets with their father Zebedee. This was a family business large enough to have hired men {Mark 1:20}.

They left the boat and their father and followed Him. There was a cost to following: job, security, and family expectations.

A Deeper Look · Following with a Cost

This scene is not a command to abandon one's family. It is a confession that Jesus is above everything. Later, Zebedee's wife also became one of those who followed Jesus (27:56).

4 · Matthew 4:23–25

Teaching, Preaching, Healing

Jesus traveled all over Galilee, teaching in their , preaching the of the kingdom of heaven, and healing every kind of disease.

The news spread to Syria, the Decapolis, and across the Jordan. Jews and Gentiles came together.

A Deeper Look · The Structure of the Gospel of Matthew

4:23 and 9:35 are almost the exact same sentence. Between them come Jesus' “teaching” (chapters 5–7, the Sermon on the Mount) and “healings” (chapters 8–9, miracles). By showing word and deed together, Matthew proves who Jesus is.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. How does the fact that Jesus sought me “first” help me look at my faith differently?
  2. What is the “net” I need to leave behind to follow Jesus?
  3. What would it look like if I used what I am good at for the kingdom of God?
  4. Who is a friend around me “sitting in darkness”? How can I be a light?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for first seeking and calling people whom no one else would choose. Help me to leave the nets that hold me back and follow You. Let me use the things I am good at to lead people to salvation. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ, who called me. Amen.
