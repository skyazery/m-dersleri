Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 23

The Community of the Little Ones and Forgiveness

Matthew 18:1–35

The fourth sermon in the Gospel of Matthew is the 'community sermon'. The disciples were arguing about 'who is the greatest', but Jesus places the little ones in the center, describing a community that seeks out the one who has gone astray and forgives endlessly.

Background

We understand the meaning of 'greatness' and 'little ones' in the kingdom of heaven.

Main idea

We can explain the parable of the lost sheep and the procedure for community restoration (Matthew 18:15–20).

Application

We identify someone to forgive through the parable of the unforgiving servant.

1 · Matthew 18:1–9

He put a child in the middle

Jesus had predicted His suffering twice, but the disciples were asking, 'Who is the greatest?' Jesus puts a child 'in the middle'. At that time, children had neither legal rights nor status.

“Whoever himself like this child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven.” Then He gives a severe warning to those who cause the little ones to stumble: it would be better for them to have a millstone hung around their neck and be thrown into the sea.

Looking Deeper · 'Change'

“Unless you change and become like little children.” Even those who are already disciples need to 'change'. The main characteristic of a child is 'dependence' rather than innocence. It is knowing you cannot stand on your own and leaning on the Father.

2 · Matthew 18:10–14

Ninety-nine and one

While the lost sheep in Luke 15 is about a 'lost sinner', the lost sheep in Matthew 18 is about a little one who has 'gone astray' within the church. It refers to a weak brother or sister in the community.

“Your Father in heaven does not want any of these little ones to perish.” By the numbers, 99% remain, but for God, the 'one' is everything.

Looking Deeper · Their angels

“Their angels in heaven always see the face of my Father” (Matthew 18:10). The little ones are by no means small before God. Like courtiers who constantly see the king's face in the palace, their situation is immediately reported to God.

3 · Matthew 18:15–22

The way to win over your brother

If a brother sins, you should first speak to him one-on-one, then with one or two others, and then tell it to the church. The goal is not punishment, but to 'win over your brother'. This is the way to seek the lost sheep.

Peter asked, “Should I up to seven times?” Rabbis said three times was enough, so Peter was speaking generously. Jesus says, “Not seven times, but seventy-seven times.” This means do not keep count.

Looking Deeper · Reversing Lamech's song

In Genesis 4:24, Lamech boasted of his revenge, saying, “If Cain is avenged seven times, then Lamech seventy-seven times.” Jesus takes that number and reverses it into a number of forgiveness.

4 · Matthew 18:23–35

Ten thousand talents and a hundred denarii

The servant's debt was ten thousand . Since one talent was about 15–20 years' wages for a laborer, ten thousand talents was an amount he could not pay even if he worked a thousand lifetimes. His fellow servant's debt to him was a hundred denarii, about three months' wages.

The king had mercy and forgave it all, but he grabbed his fellow servant by the throat. “Shouldn't you have had mercy on your fellow servant just as I had on you?” Only a person who knows the greatness of the forgiveness they received from God can forgive someone else.

Looking Deeper · From the heart

“Unless you forgive your brother from your heart.” Forgiveness starts with a decision, not a feeling. Even if the wound doesn't heal immediately, it is the decision to tear up the debt paper before God. Forgiving does not mean saying the wrong was 'okay'.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Who is the 'little one' we need to place 'in the middle' in our church or group?
  2. Has anyone ever come looking for me when I was like a lost sheep?
  3. When there is a conflict, do I speak 'one-on-one', or do I tell others first?
  4. If there is someone who is hard to forgive 'from the heart', what could be the first step?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Our Father in heaven, thank You for not wanting anyone to perish and for coming to look for me when I went astray. Instead of arguing about 'who is the greatest', let me be someone who places the little ones in the center. Remembering the ten thousand talent forgiveness I received, help me to forgive my brother from my heart. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
