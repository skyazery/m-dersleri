Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 16

Lord of the Sabbath

Matthew 12:1–50

The Sabbath was originally a gift of freedom given to Israel, who had been slaves. But over time, it turned into a list of rules that oppressed people. Jesus says: I am the Lord of the Sabbath.

Background

We understand the background of the Sabbath controversy and the true meaning of the Sabbath that Jesus taught.

Main idea

We can explain the picture of Jesus seen in the 'Song of the Servant' in Isaiah 42.

Application

We apply the concepts of words and fruit, and our identity as God's family to our lives.

1 · Matthew 12:1–8

Greater than the temple

The Pharisees considered picking grain as 'reaping' and rubbing it as 'threshing,' calling this a violation of the . The rabbis had divided forbidden work into 39 categories.

Jesus gives the example of David eating the consecrated bread and the priests who work on the Sabbath. Then He declares: “One greater than the temple is here,” and “The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.”

A Deeper Look · 11:28 and 12:1

The Sabbath controversy comes immediately after the words “I will give you rest.” True rest is found in Jesus, not in rules. Following 9:13, Jesus again recalls Hosea 6:6 (“I desire mercy, and not sacrifice”).

2 · Matthew 12:9–14

It is lawful to do good

Looking for a reason to accuse Him, the Pharisees asked, “Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath?” According to the rules of that time, if a life was not in danger, medical treatment had to be postponed to the next day.

Jesus gave the example of a sheep falling into a pit and said, “It is lawful to do good on the Sabbath,” and He healed the hand. But the Pharisees went out and plotted how they might kill Jesus. Jesus, who gives life on the Sabbath, is contrasted with those who plot to kill on the Sabbath.

A Deeper Look · The purpose of the rule

Rules exist to keep people alive. If we lose the person while keeping the rule, we have lost the purpose of the rule. Jesus didn't break the Law; He restored the heart of the Law (5:17).

3 · Matthew 12:15–37

The Song of the Servant

Matthew quotes at length from Isaiah 42:1–4: “He will not quarrel nor cry out... A bruised He will not break, and a smoldering wick He will not snuff out, till He leads justice to victory. In His name the nations will put their hope.”

But the Pharisees called Jesus's driving out of demons the power of . Jesus said, “Make a tree good and its fruit will be good,” explaining that the mouth speaks what the heart is full of. On the day of judgment, people will have to give an account for every empty word.

A Deeper Look · Blasphemy against the Holy Spirit

The “blasphemy against the Holy Spirit” in 12:31–32 is not an accidental slip of the tongue. It is a heart that sees the clear work of the Holy Spirit, deliberately calls it evil, and rejects it to the end. A person who is worried about committing this is already not in that state.

4 · Matthew 12:38–50

The sign of Jonah and the new family

When the people ask for a sign, Jesus says: “No sign will be given to it except the sign of Jonah.” Just as Jonah was in the belly of the huge fish for three days, the Son of Man will be in the heart of the earth for three days. This is the first announcement of the resurrection.

When His mother and brothers arrive, Jesus points to His disciples and says: “Whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother.” It is a new bound not by blood, but by obedience.

A Deeper Look · Neglecting His family?

Even on the cross, Jesus entrusted His mother to John (John 19:26–27). He didn't neglect His family; He showed that the family of the kingdom of heaven is much broader. Anyone who feels like an orphan can enter this family.

Discussion questions

  1. Is the Lord's Day a 'gift of rest' for me, or a 'rule to be kept'?
  2. Have I ever lost sight of a person while trying to keep a rule?
  3. When I was like a 'bruised reed,' was there a person or event that held me up instead of breaking me?
  4. When was a moment that I felt the church was a 'family'?

My confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, Lord of the Sabbath, thank You for being the one who gives rest and life, not rules. Do not break me when I am like a bruised reed, and rekindle my smoldering faith. Let me live as Your family, doing the Father's will. I pray in Jesus Christ's name. Amen.
