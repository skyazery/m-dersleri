Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 18

A Kingdom Worth Everything

Matthew 13:24–58

If God's kingdom has come, why is there still evil in the world? Why does it look so small and slow? Jesus answers these questions with six short parables and finally asks us: would you give up everything for this kingdom?

Background

We understand the present appearance of God's kingdom through the parables of the weeds, the mustard seed, and the yeast.

Main idea

We can explain what the parables of the treasure and the pearl say about the value of the kingdom.

Application

We examine how we treat Jesus by looking at His rejection in Nazareth.

1 · Matthew 13:24–30, 36–43

Why are there weeds?

(poisonous darnel) were almost indistinguishable from wheat until they formed heads. Their roots were also tangled together; if you pulled one, the other would come out too.

Jesus explains: the field is the world, the good seed are the children of the kingdom, the weeds are the children of the evil one, the enemy is the devil, and the harvest is the end of the age. They are mixed now, but God gives the judgment.

A Deeper Look · No hasty judgment

The servants' zeal (“should we pull them up?”) looks good, but the owner stops them. Humans cannot fully know who is wheat and who is a weed. The church strives for purity, but must leave the final judgment to God (7:1).

2 · Matthew 13:31–35

A small and hidden beginning

The was a symbol of the “smallest thing” at that time, but in Galilee it grew over 3 meters tall. In the parable of the yeast, the three measures of flour the woman mixed in was about 40 kg, enough bread to feed over a hundred people.

God's kingdom, starting with twelve ordinary disciples, seemed insignificant. But it grows silently, from the inside, and unceasingly. Churches all over the world today are proof of this.

A Deeper Look · Birds build nests

In Ezekiel 17:23 and Daniel 4, a “big tree where birds build their nests” is a symbol of a kingdom encompassing all nations. The parable of the mustard seed foretells God's kingdom embracing the Gentiles too.

3 · Matthew 13:44–46

Joyfully selling everything

In an era of frequent wars, it was common to bury valuables in the ground. A hired worker accidentally found a while plowing a field. A merchant, on the other hand, searched for good pearls all his life and found the best one.

One found it by chance, the other after a long search. But both sold “everything” they had. The key is not sacrifice, but “joy”: “in his joy he goes and sells all that he has.”

A Deeper Look · A trade, not a loss

The man who bought the treasure did not suffer a loss; he made the best trade of his life. What we leave behind while following Jesus is not a loss, but the natural response of someone who has gained something much greater (Philippians 3:8).

4 · Matthew 13:47–58

The net and Nazareth

The parable of the net, like the parable of the weeds, speaks of the separation on the last day. Jesus says: “Every teacher of the law who has become a disciple in the kingdom of heaven is like the master of a house who brings out of his treasure things new and old.”

Then He went to His hometown of Nazareth, but the people Him, saying, “Isn't this the carpenter's son?” Because He was so familiar, they didn't recognize the treasure. Jesus didn't do many miracles there because of their unbelief.

A Deeper Look · The trap of familiarity

A person who grows up in the church can also become like the people of Nazareth. The story of Jesus becomes so familiar to us that we are not moved. Perhaps we are the ones who walk over the treasure hidden in the field every day without seeing it.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Have you ever been disappointed because “weeds” are mixed in the world (or the church)? What perspective does this parable give?
  2. Is there a trace of faith in my life that started small like a “mustard seed” but is growing?
  3. Have you ever felt Jesus as a “treasure worth selling everything for”? If not, what is covering it?
  4. Am I a “Nazarene” who has become too used to the story of Jesus?

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for showing God's kingdom, which, although it seems small and hidden, will eventually fill the whole world. Let me not be one who judges the weeds, but one who grows like good wheat. Above all, let me recognize You as a treasure hidden in a field and joyfully give up everything for You. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
