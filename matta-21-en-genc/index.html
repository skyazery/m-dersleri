Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 21

Confession on the Rock

Matthew 16:1–28

This is the turning point of the Gospel of Matthew. If until now the question was 'Who is this?', here comes the disciples' answer. And at that very moment, the direction of the story turns toward the cross. Following comes after confession.

Background

We understand the meaning of Peter's confession in the whole Gospel of Matthew.

Main idea

We can explain the connection between Christ's announcement of suffering and discipleship.

Application

We give our own answer to the question 'Who do you say I am?' and decide to follow Him.

1 · Matthew 16:1–12

You read the sky, but...

The Pharisees and Sadducees were actually enemies, but they came together to test Jesus. Jesus says, 'You know how to discern the appearance of the sky, but you can't discern the signs of the times,' and tells them He will only give them the sign of Jonah.

The disciples were worried because they forgot the bread. Jesus says, 'You of little faith,' and reminds them of two miracles. The real danger was not hunger, but their , that is, their teaching.

Look Deeper · Remembering faith

Even though the disciples saw the miracle twice, they still worried. Faith grows more by 'remembering' the grace we have already received than by new miracles. Jesus asks, 'Don't you remember?'

2 · Matthew 16:13–20

The question at Caesarea Philippi

Caesarea Philippi was a pagan city with a temple to the god Pan and a temple to the emperor on a huge rock cliff. In that place where gods and the emperor competed, Jesus asks, 'But who do you say I am?'

Peter's answer: 'You are the , the Son of the living God.' Jesus says, 'You are . On this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades will not overcome it,' and He gives him the keys to the kingdom of heaven.

Look Deeper · What is the rock?

There is a play on words between 'Peter' (Petros) and 'rock' (petra). Church traditions interpret differently whether the rock is Peter himself or his confession. What is clear is that the church is built on the confession 'Jesus is the Christ,' through the people who make this confession.

3 · Matthew 16:21–23

Get behind Me, Satan

'From that time on,' Jesus openly says for the first time that He will suffer, die, and rise again in Jerusalem. This is the second turning point in the Gospel of Matthew, pairing with the 'From that time on' in Matt. 4:17.

Peter, who had just been praised, took hold of Jesus and objected. Jesus rebukes him, 'Get behind Me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to Me.' The rock suddenly turned into a stumbling block. A Messiah without a cross was the path Satan offered in the wilderness (Matt. 4:8–10).

Look Deeper · The things of God and the things of men

Peter's words came out of love. But even good intentions can hinder God's will. Confessing Jesus as the 'Christ' and accepting 'what kind of Christ' He is are different things.

4 · Matthew 16:24–28

Taking up your cross

'If anyone desires to come after Me, let him , take up his cross, and follow Me.' The cross was a Roman tool of execution. The condemned carried their own cross to the place of execution.

'For what will it profit a person if he gains the whole world, and forfeits his life?' It is a paradox. Whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses it for Jesus will find it.

Look Deeper · Self-denial

Self-denial is not hating oneself, but handing over the steering wheel of life to Jesus. It is not throwing away 'me', but throwing away 'the life where I am the master'.

Discussion questions

  1. Who do people (friends, social media) say Jesus is today?
  2. If Jesus asked me right now, 'Who do you say I am?', what would I want to answer?
  3. Have I ever tried to hinder God's will with good intentions like Peter?

Closing prayer
