Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 7

Upside-down happiness

Matthew 5:1–16

The world says that the rich, the strong, and those who laugh are happy. But on the mountain, Jesus presented the exact opposite list: the poor, those who weep, and the persecuted are blessed.

Background

We understand the structure and meaning of the Beatitudes, which are the beginning of the Sermon on the Mount.

Main idea

We can explain that 'blessedness' is a gift of the kingdom of heaven.

Application

We determine the life we will show in our own place as salt and light.

1 · Matthew 5:1–2

New Moses, new mountain

Seeing the crowds, Jesus went up on a and sat down. In those days, teachers sat to teach. This showed that an authoritative teaching was beginning.

Matthew's readers would remember Moses on Mount Sinai in this scene. Just as Moses received the Law on the mountain, Jesus teaches the life of the kingdom of heaven on the mountain.

Looking deeper · Five great sermons

There are five great sermons in the Gospel of Matthew (chapters 5–7, 10, 13, 18, 24–25). This structure brings to mind the five books of the Torah. The Sermon on the Mount is the first and most famous of them.

2 · Matthew 5:3–12

Upside-down happiness

“ are the , for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.” Jesus declares “blessed” eight times.

This is not a list of conditions saying, “if you do this, you will be blessed.” It is a declaration of stating that the kingdom of heaven is already open to such people.

Looking deeper · Beginning and end

The first (5:3) and eighth (5:10) beatitudes end with the same words: “the kingdom of heaven is theirs.” All the blessings within this frame are about the kingdom of heaven. The first four are about poverty before God, and the last four are about a life directed toward one's neighbor.

3 · Matthew 5:13

Salt that has lost its flavor

“You are the of the world.” Salt was a valuable thing that gave flavor and protected meat from spoiling.

The salt from near the Dead Sea contained impurities, and when its saltiness was gone, it became useless. When a disciple of Jesus becomes exactly like the world, they turn into salt that has lost its flavor.

Looking deeper · “You are”

Jesus does not say “become salt,” He says “you are salt.” This is an identity already given. The issue is not whether to be salt or not, but not to lose the flavor.

4 · Matthew 5:14–16

A light that cannot be hidden

“You are the of the world. A city set on a hill cannot be hidden.” The villages on the Galilean hills could be seen from far away even at night.

A small clay lamp would light up an entire one-room house. People who see our good deeds the Father in heaven. This is not a light that boasts of itself, but a light that points to God.

Looking deeper · Tension with 6:1

5:16 says “let them see,” while 6:1 says “do not do them to be seen.” This is not a contradiction. The difference is in the purpose. If God is glorified, it is light; if I want to receive praise, it is a show.

Discussion questions

  1. Which of the Beatitudes touches your heart the most right now? Why?
  2. How is the happiness the world talks about different from the blessedness Jesus talks about?
