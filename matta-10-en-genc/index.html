Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 10

What to seek first

Matthew 6:19–34

Grades, the future, money, appearance, family. The worries of teenagers are heavier than we think. Instead of scolding us for worrying, Jesus told us to change our perspective: look at the birds in the sky and the flowers in the field.

Tap the underlined words: meaning, original-language form and Azerbaijani word.

Background

We understand the meaning of storing up treasure in heaven and the two masters.

Main idea

We can explain the root of worry and the care of our heavenly Father.

Application

We make “seeking first” the priority of our daily lives.

1 · Matthew 6:19–21

Where your treasure is

At that time, wealth consisted of expensive clothes, grain, and money. A would eat the clothes, insects would eat the grain, and thieves would break through mud walls and enter the house.

“For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” If we look at what we spend our time and money on, we will see where our heart is.

A Deeper Look · Treasure in heaven

Treasure in heaven is not just a reward received after death. Helping the poor (19:21), loving God, and leading people to salvation already carry eternal value right now.

2 · Matthew 6:22–24

The eye and two masters

“The lamp of the body is the eye.” In Hebrew idioms, a “healthy eye” meant generosity, while a “bad eye” meant stinginess.

“No one can serve two masters. You cannot serve both God and .” Here, “to serve” means that a slave belongs to his master.

A Deeper Look · Mammon

“Wealth” is “mammon” in Aramaic. Jesus names money as a person competing with God. Money is a good servant, but a bad master.

3 · Matthew 6:25–30

Birds and wildflowers

“Don't for your life, what you will eat, or what you will drink; nor yet for your body, what you will wear.” Jesus tells us to look at the birds in the sky and the lilies in the field.

“Which of you by being anxious can add one moment to his lifespan?” Worry changes nothing; it only takes away the joy of today.

A Deeper Look · “How much more?”

Jesus's logic goes from “lesser to greater.” If God clothes even the grass that is here today and thrown into the oven tomorrow, how much more will He care for His children. The opposite of worry is not irresponsibility, but trust.

4 · Matthew 6:31–34

His Kingdom first

The pagans run after all these things, but your heavenly Father knows that you need them. “But seek first God's Kingdom and His .”

“Therefore don't be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day's own evil is sufficient.”

A Deeper Look · Connection to the Lord's Prayer

“Seek first the Kingdom” is connected to “Let Your Kingdom come” in the Lord's Prayer, and “knows your needs” is connected to “Give us our daily bread.” Turning worry into prayer is Jesus's way (Phil. 4:6).

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. What do I spend my time and money on the most? What does this say about my heart?
  2. When does money stop being a “good servant” and become a “master”?
  3. Share your biggest worry these days. What happens if we apply the “how much more” logic to it?
  4. How can I practically live out “seeking first” this week?

My confession

Closing prayer

My heavenly Father, I believe that You, who feed the birds and dress the wildflowers, care for me even more. Free me from the worries that divide my heart and from the mastery of money. Help me to live today by seeking Your Kingdom and righteousness first. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
