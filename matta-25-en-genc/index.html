Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 25

The Math of Grace

Matthew 20:1–34

“That's not fair!” The one who worked all day and the one who worked for one hour got the same money. The kingdom of heaven turns our math upside down. And at the peak of that upside-down math stands the King who came to serve.

Tap the underlined words: meaning, original-language form and Azerbaijani word.

Background

We understand the difference between grace and fairness in the parable of the vineyard workers.

Main idea

We can compare the world's leadership with Jesus' servant leadership.

Application

We understand the meaning of the word 'ransom' and determine our place of service.

1 · Matthew 20:1–7

Five Calls

During the grape harvest, it was necessary to harvest quickly before it rained, so there was a shortage of workers. The owner goes to the marketplace at 6 a.m., 9 a.m., 12 p.m., 3 p.m., and even at 5 p.m., one hour before sunset.

Those standing at 5 p.m. said, “No one has hired us.” They were probably people who were not chosen because they were old or weak. The owner called them too.

Look Deeper · The Owner Who Goes Seeking

Usually, the worker looks for the owner, but this owner goes out to seek them himself five times. This is an image of God, like the Shepherd in chapter 9 and the shepherd seeking the lost sheep in chapter 18.

2 · Matthew 20:8–16

The Last Will Be First

The owner deliberately gave the wages starting from the last ones to arrive so everyone could see. When the one-hour worker received a , the morning workers got their hopes up, but they also received one denarius. They grumbled, “You made them equal to us.”

The owner's answer: “Friend, I am doing you no wrong. … Or is your envious because I am generous?” The morning worker lost nothing. The problem is a heart that cannot rejoice in the grace someone else receives.

Look Deeper · The Prodigal Son's Brother

The morning worker in this parable is like the older brother of the prodigal son in Luke 15. A person who has been in the faith for a long time might envy the grace shown to a newcomer. This is also an answer to Peter's question in 19:27 (“What will we get?”).

3 · Matthew 20:17–28

The Right and Left Sides

Jesus foretells His suffering for the third time and in the most detail: He will be handed over, condemned, mocked by the Gentiles, flogged, crucified, and raised on the third day. Right after this, the mother of Zebedee's sons asks for high places.

Jesus asks: “Are you able to drink the that I am about to drink?” Then He says: “You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them. But it shall not be so among you. Whoever desires to become great among you shall be your servant.”

Look Deeper · Ransom

A 'ransom' (lutron) is the price paid to free a slave or captive. Jesus came to give His life as this price. This is the fulfillment of the Suffering Servant in Isaiah 53. The peak of service is the cross.

4 · Matthew 20:29–34

What Do You Want?

Jericho is the last gate on the road going up to Jerusalem. Two men by the road shouted, “Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!” When the crowd rebuked them, they shouted even louder.

Jesus asks: “What do you want me to do for you?” This is the same question He asked the mother in 20:21. The mother asked for power, while the blind men asked to see. Those whose eyes were opened followed Jesus. This was the road leading to the cross.

Look Deeper · Who Is Truly Blind?

The disciples who argued over positions even after hearing the prophecy of suffering three times, and the blind men who recognized Jesus as the 'Son of David' despite their physical blindness. Who is the one who truly sees?

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. In the parable of the vineyard, do I relate more to the morning worker or the 5 p.m. worker?
  2. Have I ever been unable to rejoice in a blessing or praise someone else received?
  3. Have you seen 'the leadership of the Gentiles' and 'servant leadership' at school or in a group?
  4. If Jesus asked me right now, 'What do you want?', what would I ask for?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, who came to serve, I thank You for Your love in giving Your own life as the price for my life. Let me not envy the grace someone else receives, but rejoice together with them. Instead of asking for a high place, let my eyes be opened to see You, and let me follow You in the place of service. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
