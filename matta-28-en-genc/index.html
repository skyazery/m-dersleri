Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 28

Humility and Watchfulness

Matthew 23:1–24:51

Jesus' final public sermon begins with sharp warnings and tears. Seven times He says “woe to you” against hypocrisy, followed by a lament for Jerusalem. Then, on the Mount of Olives, He speaks of the future and commands one thing: keep watch.

Background

We understand the reason Jesus rebuked the hypocrisy of the Pharisees and the principle of humility.

Main idea

We can explain the core message of the fifth sermon in the Gospel of Matthew (the end-times sermon).

Application

We apply 'watchfulness' to our daily lives with faithfulness, not fear.

1 · Matthew 23:1–12

Those Who Speak but Do Not Act

Jesus says that the teachers of the law and the Pharisees “sit on Moses' seat”, so obey their teaching, but do not follow their example. “For they say, and don't do.”

They make their broad, lengthen the fringes of their garments, and love the place of honor at feasts and the title “Rabbi”. Jesus says, “You have one Teacher, and you are all brothers,” reiterating that the greatest is the one who serves (20:26).

A Deeper Look · Seven “Woe to You”s

In 23:13–36, “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!” is repeated seven times. This structure contrasts with the Beatitudes in chapter 5. There are strong metaphors like straining out a gnat and swallowing a camel, a cup clean on the outside but dirty inside, and whitewashed tombs.

2 · Matthew 23:37–39

The Wings of a Mother Hen

At the end of the seven warnings, Jesus' tone changes. “Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets, and stones those who are sent to her!” Behind the anger were tears.

“How often I would have gathered your children together, even as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, and you !” God does not embrace by force. He respects even the freedom to reject the invitation.

A Deeper Look · God's Maternal Care

The Bible also depicts God as an eagle (Deut. 32:11) and a mother (Isa. 66:13). The image of taking refuge under wings frequently appears in the Psalms (91:4). Jesus applies this embrace of God to Himself.

3 · Matthew 24:1–31

Not One Stone on Another

Some of the stones of Herod's temple were over 12 meters long. When the disciples marveled, Jesus says: “There will not be left here one stone on another, that will not be thrown down.” This prophecy was fulfilled exactly as spoken by the Roman army in AD 70.

On the Mount of Olives, Jesus speaks of false christs, wars, famines, earthquakes, and persecutions, saying: “These things must happen, but the end is not yet,” and “these are the beginning of birth pains.” And: “This Good News of the Kingdom will be preached in the whole world for a testimony to all the nations, and then the end will come.”

A Deeper Look · Reading the End-Times Sermon

In chapter 24, the near future (the destruction of the temple in AD 70) and the distant future (Jesus' second coming) overlap. The purpose is not to calculate dates, but to not be deceived, not fear, endure to the end, and spread the Gospel.

4 · Matthew 24:32–51

The Faithful and Wise Servant

“But no one knows of that day and hour, not even the angels of heaven, nor the Son, but the Father alone.” Just as in the days of Noah, people will be eating and drinking when it suddenly comes upon them. Therefore, “.”

Keeping watch is not staring at the sky all night. The servant who gives the household “their food in due season” as entrusted by the master is the watchful servant. He is contrasted with the servant who beats his fellow servants and drinks with drunkards because the master is delayed.

A Deeper Look · The Lesson of the Fig Tree

Unlike the cursed tree in chapter 21, the fig tree in 24:32 is a sign of hope. Just as summer is near when its branch becomes tender and puts out its leaves, the completion of the kingdom of heaven will surely come.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Is there anything in my faith that is just for show, like “making phylacteries broad”?
  2. What does Jesus weeping while being angry tell us about God's heart?
  3. When I hear about the end times, am I afraid or expectant? Why?
  4. If Jesus came today, what would I want Him to find me doing?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for Your heart that wept for Jerusalem even as You rebuked hypocrisy. Let me live with a serving faith, not one for show. Let me return under the wings of the hen, wait for Your coming with hope instead of fear, and be faithful in the work You have entrusted to me. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
