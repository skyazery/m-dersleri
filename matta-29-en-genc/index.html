Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 29

What is waiting filled with?

Matthew 25:1–46

After saying 'Stay awake,' Jesus explains its meaning with three stories: prepared waiting (the ten girls), working waiting (the talents), and serving waiting (the sheep and the goats). Waiting is not standing still; it is the busiest time of love.

Background

We understand how the three parables in chapter 25 make 'staying awake' concrete.

Main idea

We can explain the difference between faithfulness and fear in the parable of the talents.

Application

We identify concrete ways to serve 'the least of these'.

1 · Matthew 25:1–13

Prepared waiting

In those days, a wedding began at night with the bridegroom coming to the bride's house to take her. The bride's friends waited with . It was unknown when the bridegroom would arrive.

All ten girls fell asleep. The difference was not in staying awake, but in being 'prepared.' Oil could not be borrowed. Faith and preparation cannot be borrowed from someone else; they belong to each person.

Looking deeper · What is the oil?

Traditionally, it is interpreted in various ways, such as the Holy Spirit, good deeds, or faith. If we connect it with 7:21–23, it is not just saying 'Lord, Lord,' but a life that does the will of the Father. The bridegroom's words 'I don't know you' are also the same as in 7:23.

2 · Matthew 25:14–18

What is entrusted

One was about 20 years' wages for a laborer. Even the servant who received the least received a huge amount. The master entrusted it 'to each according to his own ability.' The amount is different, but the expectation is the same.

The servants who received five and two talents 'went at once' and traded. The servant who received one talent dug in the ground. According to rabbinic law at the time, burying money in the ground was the safest way against theft. His goal was not to lose it.

Looking deeper · The word 'talent'

The English word 'talent' (ability) comes exactly from this parable. But the talent in the parable is not just ability; it is everything God has entrusted to us: the Gospel, time, relationships, and opportunities.

3 · Matthew 25:19–30

A talent buried in fear

Two servants receive the same praise: 'Well done, good and servant! You were faithful over a few things, I will set you over many things. Enter into the joy of your lord!' It is not the size of the result that is praised, but the faithfulness.

The third servant says: 'You are a hard man ... I was afraid, and went away and hid your talent in the earth.' A wrong idea about the master bred fear, and fear caused him to do nothing.

Looking deeper · Scarier than failure

There is no 'servant who lost money in business' in this parable. God regrets the one who was afraid and did nothing more than the one who tried and failed. A person who knows the God of grace can take the risk of failure and try.

4 · Matthew 25:31–46

The least of these

The Son of Man will come in His glory and separate all nations as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats. Surprisingly, the criterion is not religious knowledge, but how they treated the hungry, the thirsty, the strangers, the naked, the sick, and those in prison.

Both the sheep and the goats are surprised: 'Lord, when...?' Those who served did not know they were doing it to Jesus. Because they loved without calculating. Jesus identifies Himself with the .

Looking deeper · Salvation by works?

This scene does not mean 'being saved by doing good works.' A person who receives the King's grace naturally loves the least of these. The fruit proves the root (7:17). The entire Gospel of Matthew speaks of a faith where word and deed are one.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Do I have enough 'oil' of faith? What is my preparation that I cannot borrow from someone else?
  2. If I were to list three 'talents' God has entrusted to me, what would they be?
  3. Is there a talent I have buried out of fear? Something I haven't tried because I was afraid of failing?
  4. Who is the 'least of these' around me? If I saw Jesus in their face, how would I treat them?

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for teaching me that waiting is not standing still, but a time filled with love.
