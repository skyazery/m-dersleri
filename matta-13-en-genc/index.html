Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 13

Those Who Need a Doctor

Matthew 9:1–38

Who you ate with showed who you were. Jesus intentionally sat at the table with people others said 'you can't eat with.' And He said: I am a doctor, I have come for the sick.

Background

We understand that Jesus has the authority to forgive sins.

Main idea

We can explain the calling of Matthew and the meaning of the word 'mercy'.

Application

We pray for the person we will carry in our hearts as harvest workers.

1 · Matthew 9:1–8

The Authority to Forgive Sins

Seeing the faith of the friends, Jesus first said to the man, 'Your sins are forgiven.' The teachers of the law thought to themselves, 'This is blasphemy.' Because only God can forgive sins.

Jesus said, 'But that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins,' and made him walk. The visible healing proved the invisible forgiveness.

Looking Deeper · Which is easier?

The words 'Your sins are forgiven' cannot be verified, so they seem easy to say. But 'Get up and walk' is immediately verified. By doing what seemed harder, Jesus showed that He could also do what is truly hard.

2 · Matthew 9:9–13

From the Tax Booth to the Table

A was someone who collected taxes from his own people for Rome and Herod. He was considered a traitor and a fraud, and was shunned in the synagogue. The name of this Gospel is that very tax collector, Matthew.

When the Pharisees asked, 'Why does He eat with tax collectors and sinners?', Jesus quoted Hosea 6:6: 'I desire , and not sacrifice.'

Looking Deeper · Matthew's humility

Mark and Luke call him 'Levi', but the Gospel of Matthew uses the name 'Matthew' and introduces him as 'Matthew the tax collector' in the list of disciples in 10:3. It is a confession that boasts of grace without hiding his past.

3 · Matthew 9:14–26

New Wine and a Touch

Jesus compared Himself to a bridegroom and said that new wine must be put into new wineskins. The kingdom of heaven that Jesus brought cannot fit into old molds.

A woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years was considered 'unclean' according to the Law and could not touch people. When she touched the fringe of Jesus's cloak, Jesus did not let her hide; He called her 'daughter' and restored her publicly.

Looking Deeper · Two daughters

The stories of the woman who had been sick for twelve years and the twelve-year-old girl (Mark 5:42) are woven together. Both were associated with 'uncleanness' (blood, a dead body), but Jesus touched both and restored both as 'daughters'.

4 · Matthew 9:27–38

Harvest Workers

Jesus raised the dead girl to life, and healed the blind and the mute. Chapters 8 and 9 end with the same sentence (9:35) as 4:23.

When Jesus saw the crowds, He on them because they were 'harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd.' 'The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few. Ask the Lord of the harvest, therefore, to send out workers into His harvest field.'

Looking Deeper · A heart that aches

'Had compassion' (splanchnizomai) comes from the word for 'internal organs'. It is a sympathy so deep that you feel an ache in your gut. This heart leads to the sending out of the disciples in the next chapter (Chapter 10).

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Has anyone ever brought me to Jesus, like the friends of the paralyzed man?
  2. Who is shunned like 'Matthew' in our school or neighborhood? Can I sit at that table?
  3. Have I ever prioritized 'sacrifice' (form) over 'mercy'?
  4. Who is one person I will carry in my heart with prayer as a 'harvest worker'?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, thank You for being the Doctor who came to call not the righteous, but sinners. Let me not forget that I too am a called sinner, just like Matthew. Give me a heart that has compassion on my friends who are like sheep without a shepherd, and use me as a harvest worker. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
