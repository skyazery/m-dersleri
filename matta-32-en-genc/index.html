Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 32

Truly the Son of God

Matthew 27:1–66

All roads in the Gospel of Matthew led to this hill. The One born as King hangs under the sign “King of the Jews”. In that place where everyone mocked and abandoned Him, the most unexpected person confesses: “Truly, this was the Son of God.”

Background

We understand the meaning of substitutionary atonement in Pilate's trial and the Barabbas incident.

Main idea

We can explain what Jesus' cry on the cross and the signs at that moment mean.

Application

We find our place before the cross and respond with thanksgiving and confession.

1 · Matthew 27:1–26

Instead of Barabbas

When Judas saw that Jesus was condemned, he regretted it and returned the thirty pieces of silver. The chief priests bought the potter's field with that “blood money”. Meanwhile, the Roman governor asked, “Are You the King of the Jews?” Jesus said, “You say so,” and remained silent.

Even Pilate's wife said, “Have nothing to do with that righteous man.” But the crowd shouted, “Barabbas!” “Barabbas” means “son of the father”. The sinful “son of the father” was set free, and the sinless “Son of the Father” died.

A Deeper Look · Pilate washing his hands

Pilate washed his hands with water and said, “I am innocent of this man's blood; see to it yourselves.” But responsibility doesn't wash away. The Apostles' Creed still says today, “suffered under Pontius Pilate.”

2 · Matthew 27:27–44

The Mocked King

The soldiers put a scarlet robe on Him, placed a crown of thorns on His head, put a reed in His hand, and mocked Him, saying, “Hail, King of the Jews!” What they did was a fake coronation, but it was actually the enthronement of the true King.

Simon of Cyrene was forced to carry the . The sign above the cross read, “Jesus, the King of the Jews.” Those passing by, the chief priests, and even the robbers mocked Him, saying, “If You are the Son of God, come down from the cross.” This is the same as Satan's temptation in the wilderness ({Matt. 4:3, 6}).

A Deeper Look · Why didn't He come down?

“He saved others, but He can't save Himself.” The mocking words were true. Because Jesus didn't save Himself, He was able to save others. He could have come down, but He stayed out of love.

3 · Matthew 27:45–50

Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani

From twelve noon until three in the afternoon, the whole land became dark. Jesus cried out with a loud voice: “Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?”, that is, “My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?”

This is {Ps. 22:1}. Jesus, bearing sin, experienced separation from the Father. Jesus was forsaken so that we would not be forsaken. Psalm 22 begins with suffering, but ends with victory and praise.

A Deeper Look · The deepest cry

In {Matt. 1:23}, Jesus came as “Immanuel, God is with us”. That Immanuel cries out on the cross, “God has forsaken Me”. Because of this, we are now never forsaken ({Matt. 28:20}).

4 · Matthew 27:51–66

The Torn Curtain

When Jesus gave up His spirit, the of the temple was torn from top to bottom. It was torn by God, not by man. Now the way to approach God was opened for everyone. The earth shook, the rocks split, and the tombs were opened.

Seeing this, the Roman and the soldiers confessed: “Truly, this was the Son of God.” In the Gospel of Matthew, the first time a person confessed this was at the foot of the cross, and it came from the mouth of a foreign soldier. The rich man Joseph placed Jesus in a new tomb, and the chief priests sealed the tomb and posted a guard.

A Deeper Look · The watching women

The disciples fled, but Mary Magdalene and the other women watched from a distance to the end ({Matt. 27:55–56}), and also sat opposite the tomb ({Matt. 27:61}). They will be the first witnesses of the resurrection.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. Which of the people at the foot of the cross in the table am I most like?
  2. When I think that I am Barabbas, what feelings do I experience?
  3. How does it comfort me that I will not be forsaken because Jesus was “forsaken”?
  4. If the way to God has been opened like the torn curtain, how often do I walk that way?

My confession

Closing prayer

Jesus, even though You were sinless, You were condemned in my place, who am like Barabbas. Even when You were mocked, You did not come down from the cross, and You were forsaken so that I would never be forsaken. I approach God through the way opened by the torn curtain. Truly, You are the Son of God. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
