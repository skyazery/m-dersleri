1 · Matthew 1:1–17

Why start with a family tree?

In the first sentence, Matthew introduces Jesus as the , the '' and the son of Abraham. For the first readers, the Jews, a was like an ID card. Because the promised Messiah absolutely had to come from David's line.

God had told Abraham, 'In you all the families of the earth will be blessed' Gen. 12:3, and to David, 'Your throne will be established forever' 2 Sam. 7:16. Matthew shows that Jesus is the owner of these two promises.

Looking deeper · The secret of 14

Hebrew letters were also used as numbers. The name David (דוד) adds up to ד(4)+ו(6)+ד(4)=14. Many scholars think that Matthew emphasizes the idea of the '' by dividing the family tree into three parts of 14 generations.

2 · Matthew 1:18

Engaged, but not yet married

At that time, a Jewish was a more serious promise than it is today. According to the law, they were already considered husband and wife, and breaking the engagement required a divorce. Usually, about a year later, the groom would take the bride to his home, and they would start living together.

3 · Matthew 1:19–21

The choice of Joseph, a righteous man

According to the law, Joseph could have brought this matter up in front of everyone. But he does not want to disgrace Mary and decides to leave secretly. The Matthew talks about is combining obedience to the law with loving a person.

Then an angel says to him in a dream, 'Joseph, son of David, don't be afraid,' and instructs him to name the baby . Because He will His people from their .

Looking deeper · The mission inside the name

The name 'Jesus' comes from the Hebrew name 'Yeshua' and means 'The Lord saves.' Many Jews were waiting for a Messiah who would free them from Roman rule. But the angel says that what Jesus will save them from is 'sin.' This means He came to solve a much deeper problem.

4 · Matthew 1:22–23

“So that the promise would be fulfilled”

Matthew says that this event happened to the word of the Isaiah Isa. 7:14. In the Gospel of Matthew, this kind of 'fulfillment' expression appears more than ten times. Matthew wants to show that Jesus' whole life is the completion of the promises in the Old Testament.

Looking deeper · Beginning and end

The Gospel of Matthew begins in 1:23 with the words 'God is with us' and ends in 28:20 with Jesus' promise, 'I am with you always, even to the end of the age.' The whole book is surrounded by the theme of 'God with us.'

5 · Matthew 1:24–25

Actions, not words

Joseph wakes up from his sleep and takes Mary to his home as the angel commanded. When the baby is born, he names him Jesus.

Not a single word spoken by Joseph is recorded in the Bible. His faith is shown by actions, not words. A father naming a child meant legally accepting that child as his own son. Because of this, Jesus becomes the 'Son of David' through Joseph.

Discussion questions

  1. How does it comfort you that Matthew included the names of four women in the family tree?
  2. If you were Joseph, how would you feel the morning after the dream? What would be the hardest part?
  3. What difference can the words 'God is with us' make at school, at home, and in friendships?
