Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 3

The Refugee King

Matthew 2:13–23

Jesus, who came as the King of the whole world, became a refugee as soon as He was born. Why did God send His Son on such a weak and dangerous path?

Background

We understand what the three prophecies quoted by Matthew mean.

Main idea

We can explain that Jesus relived the history of Israel.

Application

We reflect on what it means to believe in a God who works even in the midst of injustice and suffering.

1 · Matthew 2:13–15

A Dream Again

Joseph receives a message in a dream again and leaves that very night. Just like in the first chapter, Joseph not with words, but with actions.

Egypt was a place where Israelites had taken refuge during hard times since ancient days. At that time, there was also a large Jewish community in Alexandria.

A Deeper Look · The Son Called Out of Egypt

Matthew quotes Hosea 11:1: “I called my son out of Egypt.” Originally, this referred to Israel's . Matthew shows that Jesus is reliving the history of Israel and obeying perfectly where Israel failed.

2 · Matthew 2:16–18

Tears of Bethlehem

Herod ordered the killing of all boys in and around Bethlehem who were two years old and under. Since it was a small village, it is thought that about ten children died, but every child was their family's whole world.

Matthew quotes the words of the prophet : “ weeping for her children; she refused to be comforted.”

A Deeper Look · The Bible Doesn't Hide Sorrow

Matthew does not try to sugarcoat this tragedy. The Bible is a book that weeps with us in the face of suffering. But Jeremiah 31 promises a “new covenant” and restoration in the very same chapter. Even in the midst of tears, God's story does not end.

3 · Matthew 2:19–22

God Guiding Through Dreams

After Herod dies, an angel appears in a dream again and tells him to return to Israel. But Herod's cruel son was ruling in Judea.

When Joseph was afraid, God guided him to Galilee through another dream. In Matthew chapters 1–2 alone, God guides through dreams five times.

4 · Matthew 2:23

A Nazarene

Joseph's family settles in a small village in Galilee called . It was an ordinary village with only a few hundred people.

The saying, “Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?” John 1:46 shows that Nazareth was despised. Jesus grew up in such a place.

A Deeper Look · “A Nazarene”

This exact sentence is not written in the Old Testament. Many scholars explain it as a wordplay on the word “branch” (Hebrew netser) in Isaiah 11:1 and a reference to the despised Messiah (Isaiah 53).

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. What does the fact that Jesus was a refugee mean for people living far from their homeland today?
  2. How do I react when something unfair and sad happens even though I believe in God?
  3. Is there something I should obey “immediately” like Joseph, but I am putting it off?
  4. How does Jesus, who grew up in despised Nazareth, comfort me?

My Confession

Closing prayer

Lord God, I praise Jesus, who became a refugee and came to the lowest place. Help me to believe that Your story does not end, even in the midst of suffering and tears. Help me to be a person who obeys as soon as I hear, just like Joseph. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth. Amen.
