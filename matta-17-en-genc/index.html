Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 17

The Four Soils of the Heart

Matthew 13:1–23

Even when we listen to the same lesson, someone changes, while someone else stays the same. Is the problem with the teacher or the student? Jesus talks about the soil, not the seed. The same seed, different soils, different results.

Background

We understand the beginning of the parables sermon, which is the third sermon in the Gospel of Matthew.

Main idea

We can explain why Jesus taught in parables.

Application

We honestly examine our heart's soil and determine how to turn it into good soil.

1 · Matthew 13:1–9

The Preacher in the Boat

Jesus made the boat His pulpit and the shore His auditorium. The slopes by the Sea of Galilee formed a natural amphitheater, so His voice carried far.

A farmer would first scatter the seeds and then plow the field. Because of this, seeds also fell on the path, rocky places, and among thorns. But the harvest of the good soil was 30, 60, and 100 times what was sown. Since a normal harvest back then was 7 to 10 times, this is an astonishing result.

A Deeper Look · The Third Sermon

Chapter 13 is the third of the five sermons in the Gospel of Matthew, the 'parables sermon' right in the middle. All seven parables are about 'the kingdom of heaven.' It comes immediately after the rejection intensified in Chapter 12.

2 · Matthew 13:10–17

Why in Parables?

The disciples asked, “Why do you speak in parables?” Jesus said, “To you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it is not given,” and He quoted Isaiah 6:9–10.

A becomes a door to an open heart and a wall to a closed one. Jesus says, “Blessed are your eyes, for they see; and your ears, for they hear.” The disciples were seeing what many prophets had longed to see.

A Deeper Look · Whoever Has Will Be Given More

“For whoever has, to him will be given, and he will have abundance; but whoever doesn't have, from him will be taken away even that which he has” (13:12). This isn't about money; it's about hearing. If we respond to the Word we hear, we will understand more; if we neglect it, even what we know will become blurry.

3 · Matthew 13:18–22

The Path, the Rocky Soil, and the Thorns

The path: When someone hears the Word and doesn't understand it, the evil one comes and snatches it away. The rocky soil: receives it with joy, but since it has no , it quickly stumbles when trouble or persecution comes.

The thorny soil: hears the Word, but the of the world and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the Word, making it unfruitful. These three aren't 'bad people,' but conditions we all have.

A Deeper Look · The Most Dangerous Soil

The thorny soil is one that outwardly seems to have no problems. There is faith, and they go to church. It's just that so many other things are growing that the Word has no room to breathe. Busyness can be the quietest thorn.

4 · Matthew 13:23

A Hundredfold Harvest

“What was sown on the good soil, this is he who hears the Word and understands it, who most certainly bears fruit and produces, some a hundred times, some sixty, some thirty.”

The good soil has three characteristics: it hears, it , and it bears fruit. Even though the amount of fruit differs, they are all good soil. Growth is what matters, not comparison.

A Deeper Look · The Sower's Optimism

The sower generously scatters seed even on the path and the rocky soil. God never gives up on any heart; He sows the Word. Even if there is failure in three places, the harvest from one good soil surpasses all the losses. This parable is both a warning and a hope.

Discussion questions

Closing prayer

God who sows the seeds, I thank You for not giving up on my heart, even when it is like the path, and for sowing Your Word in it. Please clear the stones from my heart and pull out the thorns. Let me be good soil that hears the Word, understands it, and bears fruit. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
