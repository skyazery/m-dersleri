Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 5

The Son Who Overcame in the Wilderness

Matthew 4:1–11

“If You are the Son of God…” Right after the voice from heaven called Jesus the “beloved Son,” the devil tries to shake this very identity. Temptation always targets the question, “Who am I?”

Background

We understand what each of the three temptations targeted.

Main idea

We can explain that Jesus relived Israel's 40 years in the wilderness and overcame.

Application

We identify a temptation we often fall into and the Word we will hold onto at that time.

1 · Matthew 4:1–2

The Spirit Led Him to the Wilderness

It was the who led Jesus into the wilderness. The was not an accident outside of God's plan.

Jesus fasted for 40 days and 40 nights. Israel was tested in the wilderness for 40 years and often failed. Jesus walks that same path again and overcomes where Israel fell.

A Deeper Look · The Source of the Three Answers

All three of Jesus' answers are from chapters 6–8 of the book of . These are the words Moses spoke to Israel as he looked back at their 40 years in the wilderness. Jesus rewrote Israel's story of failure into a story of obedience.

2 · Matthew 4:3–4

The First Temptation · Bread

The says, “If You are the Son of God, command these stones to become bread.” Hunger itself is not a sin. The temptation was to meet His own needs by His own power without trusting the Father.

Jesus answered, “Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God” Deut. 8:3.

A Deeper Look · The “If” That Shakes Identity

“If You are the Son of God” are words that plant doubt. Just moments before, the Father had called Him the “beloved Son.” Temptation often comes in the form of “prove it.” Jesus didn't try to prove anything.

3 · Matthew 4:5–10

The Second and Third Temptations · Proof and Power

On the roof of the , the devil quotes Psalm 91 and challenges Jesus to throw Himself down. The devil can quote the Bible too. Jesus answered, “You shall not test the Lord your God” Deut. 6:16.

Finally, the devil says he will give Him all the kingdoms of the world if He worships him. This was a shortcut to glory without the cross. Jesus said, “Go away, Satan! You shall the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve” Deut. 6:13.

4 · Matthew 4:11

The Ministry of Angels

The devil left, and angels came and served Jesus. The help of the angels, which He refused in the temptation to jump from the temple, came as a gift at the end of His obedience.

Jesus was tempted in all points like we are, yet without sin Heb. 4:15. Because of this, He is able to help us when we are tempted.

A Deeper Look · “Knowing” vs. “Living” the Word

The devil also quoted the Word. What matters is not knowing many verses, but trusting God within the whole flow of the Word. Before Jesus used the Word as a weapon, He lived by it.

Closing prayer
