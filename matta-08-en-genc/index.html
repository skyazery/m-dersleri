Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 8

Deeper Righteousness

Matthew 5:17–48

“Isn't it enough if I don't break the rules?” But Jesus looked much deeper, into the heart. And He gave the hardest command: love your enemies.

Tap the underlined words: meaning, original-language form and Azerbaijani word.

Background

We understand what it means that Jesus 'fulfilled' the Law.

Main idea

We can explain the flow of the phrase 'But I tell you,' which is repeated six times.

Application

We identify a specific person in our lives to reconcile with and love.

1 · Matthew 5:17–20

Not to destroy

“Don't think that I came to destroy the or the Prophets. I didn't come to destroy, but to fulfill.”

At that time, the most zealous people were the Pharisees and the teachers of the law. Jesus said that to enter the kingdom of heaven, you need a that surpasses theirs. Not more rules, but a deeper heart.

Looking deeper · One jot, one tittle

A 'jot' is the smallest letter of the Hebrew alphabet, yodh, and a 'tittle' is a tiny stroke that distinguishes letters. Jesus didn't take the Old Testament lightly. Instead, He fulfilled the purpose the Old Testament pointed to in Himself.

2 · Matthew 5:21–26

Anger and reconciliation

To the command “Do not murder,” Jesus adds: “Everyone who is angry with his brother will be subject to judgment.” Murder starts with anger in the heart and degrading words.

He says to leave your gift in front of the and go reconcile with your brother first. Our relationship with God cannot be separated from our relationship with people.

Looking deeper · Six comparisons

In 5:21–48, “You have heard that it was said to the people long ago... But I tell you” is repeated six times. Jesus doesn't say “so-and-so said this” like the rabbis; He says “I tell you.” This is the authority of the One who gave the Law (7:29).

3 · Matthew 5:38–42

If forced to go one mile

“Eye for eye” was actually a law that limited revenge. Jesus goes a step further: “Whoever strikes you on your right cheek, turn the other to him as well.”

A Roman soldier could force a person from the occupied nation to carry a load for one mile. Jesus says to go with him two miles. Turning forced labor into voluntary goodness.

Looking deeper · Not out of weakness

This doesn't mean to just endure abuse. It is an active love that breaks the chain of revenge and surprises the other person. In a dangerous situation, asking for help is also wisdom.

4 · Matthew 5:43–48

Love your enemies

The saying “Love your neighbor and hate your enemy” is not in the Old Testament. People had added it. Jesus says: “Love your , and pray for those who persecute you.”

God makes His sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the righteous and on the unrighteous. Therefore He says: “You therefore shall be .”

Looking deeper · “Be perfect”

“Teleios” doesn't mean flawless perfection; it means “having reached the goal, mature.” When our love expands to include our enemies, we grow as children who resemble our Father in heaven.

5 questions

Discussion questions

  1. How does the idea of 'it's enough if I don't break the rules' differ from Jesus' 'surpassing righteousness'?
  2. Is there someone I need to go and reconcile with first right now? What makes me hesitate?
  3. What would 'going two miles' look like toward someone who degrades or attacks me online?
  4. What change can praying for an enemy create in my heart?

Closing prayer
