Bible lesson for teens · Matthew, lesson 33

From the End to the Beginning

Matthew 28:1–20

The Gospel of Matthew began with the words “Immanuel, God with us” (1:23). And it ends with the words “I am with you always, even to the end of the age” (28:20). The tomb was not the end, but the beginning. Now the story passes on to us.

Background

We understand the evidence and meaning of the resurrection in the flow of the Gospel of Matthew.

Main idea

We can explain the structure and core command of the Great Commission (28:18–20).

Application

Looking at the whole Gospel of Matthew, we determine our place to 'go and make disciples'.

1 · Matthew 28:1–7

He is not here

Very early on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary went to look at the tomb. With a great earthquake, an rolled back the stone and sat on it. The Roman guards became like dead men from fear.

The angel's declaration: “He is not here, for He has risen, just as He said.” The stone was rolled away not for Jesus to come out, but for us to “come and see.” The empty tomb was opened as evidence.

Looking Deeper · The first witnesses were women

At that time, the testimony of women was not accepted in court. If they had made up the resurrection story, they would not have made women the first witnesses. This is a clue showing that the record is true. The women who stayed by the cross to the end heard the first news of the resurrection.

2 · Matthew 28:8–10

To My brothers

The women ran “with fear and great joy.” At that moment, Jesus met them, saying “Greetings” (chairete, “rejoice”). The women held Jesus' feet and Him. He had risen bodily, not as a ghost.

Jesus calls the disciples “My brothers.” Even those disciples who had all fled. According to the promise in 26:32, He tells them to meet in Galilee. The resurrection is a declaration of forgiveness and restoration for the failed disciples.

Looking Deeper · The name “brother”

In 12:49–50, Jesus said that whoever does the will of the Father is “My brother.” For the first time after His resurrection, He calls the disciples brothers. Through the cross and resurrection, we became God's family.

3 · Matthew 28:11–15

The truth they tried to cover with money

Hearing the guards' report, the chief priests and elders consulted together and gave the soldiers “a large sum of money”: “Say that His disciples came by night and stole Him away while we were sleeping.”

How can a sleeping person know who stole something? The lie was contradictory from the start. But they also admitted one thing: the tomb was empty. The conspiracy that started with thirty pieces of silver tried to cover the truth with money again, but failed.

Looking Deeper · Evidences of the resurrection

The empty tomb (admitted even by enemies), the testimony of the women, more than 500 witnesses (1 Cor. 15:6), the dramatic change and martyrdom of the disciples who had fled, and the birth of the church. It is hard to explain all of this without the resurrection.

4 · Matthew 28:16–20

Go and make disciples

The eleven disciples saw Jesus on the mountain in Galilee and worshiped Him. “But some doubted.” Jesus gives the mission to everyone, without leaving out those who doubted.

“All in heaven and on earth has been given to Me. Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all things that I commanded you. Behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age.”

Looking Deeper · The structure of the Great Commission

There is one main command: “make disciples.” “Go,” “baptize,” and “teach” are the ways to do it. The “teaching” that began in the Sermon on the Mount now goes to all nations. Through the cross and resurrection, Jesus received the “authority of all the kingdoms of the world” that Satan offered to give Him in 4:8.
